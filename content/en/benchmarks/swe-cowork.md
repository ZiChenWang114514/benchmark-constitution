# SWE-CoWork

SWE-CoWork evaluates coding agents as digital employees in simulated engineering organizations: 101 tasks over 101 real repositories, each with 26 colleagues, and scoring against 2,699 fail-to-pass and 562 pass-to-pass test nodes. Across 11 models, the paper reports a best full-task pass rate of only 7.92% for GPT-6 Astra; Claude-Opus-5.5 reaches 63.84% fail-to-pass but only 6.93% complete success.

## Design goals

**Capabilities tested**：Whether a coding agent, starting from a brief handoff in a simulated engineering organization, can recover requirements distributed across colleagues and workplace records, reconcile conflicting evidence, and implement changes graded by private executable tests, completing full delivery rather than only partial behavior.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20218) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20218) [3](https://tokenwave-ai.github.io/swe-cowork-page) [4](https://github.com/TokenWave-AI/swe-cowork-bench) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Tokenwave-AI/swe-cowork) (accessed 2026-11-19)

**Gap in prior benchmarks**：The paper argues that existing coding benchmarks typically evaluate isolated, pre-scoped task descriptions, and that underspecification studies either vary information in a fixed initial request or let agents query a designated simulated user who holds the missing requirements. Agents therefore often remain specification executors rather than digital engineers who must clarify, reconcile records, and track requirements inside an organization.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20218) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20218) [3](https://tokenwave-ai.github.io/swe-cowork-page) [4](https://github.com/TokenWave-AI/swe-cowork-bench) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Tokenwave-AI/swe-cowork) (accessed 2026-11-19)

## Construction

**Question sources**：The 101 development assignments come from 101 real upstream open-source repositories, covering 11 programming languages and 12 application domains, including web frameworks, compilers, games, payments, and scientific software. Each repository is paired with an organization of 26 modeled colleagues, yielding 2,626 employee profiles across the 101 tasks. Requirements are distributed across colleagues and workplace records. The handoff states the objective, restrictions on modifying existing tests, exclusion of neighboring projects, and historical terminology that may differ from the current repository.

## Evaluation

**Metrics**：F2P: the proportion of fail-to-pass verifier test nodes satisfied by the submission; P2P: the proportion of pass-to-pass nodes preserved; pass rate: the share of tasks for which every F2P and P2P node passes in the same run. F2P and P2P are computed by pooling passed and total nodes across tasks.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20218) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20218) [3](https://tokenwave-ai.github.io/swe-cowork-page) [4](https://github.com/TokenWave-AI/swe-cowork-bench) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Tokenwave-AI/swe-cowork) (accessed 2026-11-19)

**Scoring**：Pass rate requires the full executable contract to pass in the same run. The paper also reports the share of colleague-held requirements obtained and tool-call counts. An Oracle-Spec condition supplies complete requirements up front while holding the repository, tests, scaffold, and execution budget constant.
